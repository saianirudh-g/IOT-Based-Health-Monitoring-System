# IoT-Based Health Monitoring System[cite: 1]

## Overview
The Internet of Things (IoT) is a rapidly growing technology where smart objects and devices are connected to the internet for effective communication[cite: 1]. This project aims to decrease the death rate by detecting chronic diseases such as cancer and heart diseases, and to reduce human-dependent healthcare[cite: 1]. It utilizes networked sensor devices, either worn on the body or embedded in living environments, to gather rich information to evaluate a patient's physical and mental health[cite: 1]. The system records health-related information like blood pressure, body temperature, sugar levels, and breathing patterns, delivering this data to the concerned hospital or caretaker for further action[cite: 1].

## Hardware Components
The system's core block diagram includes the following components:
*   **Microcontroller**: Arduino Uno[cite: 1]
*   **Input Sensors**: Heartbeat Sensor, Temp Sensor, and Humidity sensor[cite: 1]
*   **Output/Connectivity Modules**: LCD Display, Buzzer, and Wi-Fi Module[cite: 1]

## Sensor Categories
Medical sensors and wearables used in this system are divided into two main categories:
*   **On-body Contact Sensors**:
    *   *Monitoring sensors*: Used for physiological behavior (ECG, EMG, EEG), chemical analysis (sweat, glucose, saliva), and optical measurements (Oximetry, tissue properties)[cite: 1].
    *   *Therapeutic sensors*: Used for medication (drug delivery patches), stimulation (chronic pain relief), and emergency response (defibrillators)[cite: 1].
*   **Peripheral Non-contact Sensors**:
    *   *Monitoring fitness*: Measure fitness via motion (physical activity, calorie count) and location tracking (GPS, indoor localization)[cite: 1].
    *   *Behavioural monitoring*: Track activity (falls, sleep, exercise), emotion (anxiety, stress, depression), and diet (calorie intake, eating habits)[cite: 1].

## System Architecture
The application scenario connects the patient to stakeholders through various networking layers:
1.  Wearable Sensors attached to the patient collect biological data[cite: 1].
2.  Data is transmitted to a local Gateway[cite: 1].
3.  Information is passed through Wireless or Cellular Networks to the Internet[cite: 1].
4.  Data is securely accessed by stakeholders, including Doctors, Smart Vehicles (ambulances), and caregivers[cite: 1].

## Applications
*   **Health Monitoring**: Captures vital signs like blood pressure, blood glucose, weight, ECG, heart rate, and body temperature to monitor pediatric and aged persons[cite: 1].
*   **Personal Fitness Monitoring**: Wearable sensors help track personal fitness programs[cite: 1].
*   **Chronic Disease Monitoring**: Predicts chronic conditions such as heart attacks ahead of time[cite: 1].
*   **Medication Monitoring**: Assists in tracking and managing medication intake[cite: 1].

## Limitations and Challenges
*   **Security & Privacy Issues**: Healthcare devices and applications capture private health data and are connected to the internet for anytime, anywhere access, which may attract hackers[cite: 1].
*   **Device Designing Issues**: IoT devices used in healthcare are tiny sensors that feature low computing power processors, low storage capacity, and limited battery power[cite: 1].
*   **Trust and Reliability**: Data generated and delivered by medical devices is prone to security attacks[cite: 1]. Information might be infected or corrupted by malware during data transmission, leading caregivers to make incorrect life-and-death treatment decisions[cite: 1].
